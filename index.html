Australian International Education · 2019 to 2025

Many origins, one destination

International students in Australia, 2019 to 2025

International education is one of Australia's biggest export industries and a defining part of life in cities like Sydney and Melbourne. But “international students” is not one story. It is many. This page follows them: where they come from, where they study, and how the picture changed after the pandemic.

01

The big picture Total enrolments & new commencements, 2019–2025

From crash to record.

Over the past six years, Australia's international education sector has weathered one of the most disruptive periods in its modern history. From a pre-pandemic peak of around 950,000 enrolments, numbers fell sharply to a low of 716,000 in 2021 as the country's borders stayed closed to international students for almost two years.

By 2024, with borders fully reopened, the sector had not only recovered but reached a new record of 1.09 million students. A modest dip then followed in 2025 after the federal government proposed enrolment caps for the first time, slowing the arrival of new students.

02

Six countries, half the students

The map of origins is dominated by an "Asian crescent" running from China through the subcontinent and Southeast Asia, with a smaller cluster in South America. Africa, Europe and North America barely register. Colour is on a logarithmic scale because the gap between the leaders and the long tail is enormous.

A two-horse race, then a long tail

China (227k) and India (182k) are in a league of their own, but the more surprising finding is third place: Nepal sends nearly 90,000 students — more than Vietnam, Colombia and Indonesia combined. The chart uses a lollipop rather than a bar to keep the ink minimal while preserving the precise position that lets you read each value.

Origins are also shifting. The stream below shows the top ten sources over time: China's band is shrinking in relative terms even as the total grows, while India, the Philippines and a widening "Other" tail expand. Australian international education is becoming markedly more diversified.

03

Two states host two-thirds

Raw counts would simply rank the states by population. The map below instead shows students per 100,000 residents — combining Department of Education enrolments with ABS population. Once you control for population, a real gap appears: the student "hubs" (NSW, Victoria and tiny Canberra) sit far above the periphery, where Tasmania trails the nation.

Not all "students" are at university

Higher Education is the largest sector, but vocational education (VET) is a serious second at roughly a third of all enrolments — far bigger than most Australians assume. The remaining slices are English-language courses (ELICOS), schools and non-award study.

The sector mix is not the same everywhere. New South Wales and Victoria look like "university" states; Tasmania, Western Australia and South Australia lean noticeably more towards vocational training. There is no single international-student profile by state.

04

Not one student body — four

This is the heart of the story. Cross-tabulating origin against sector reveals that "international student" describes at least four distinct migration patterns: Chinese students concentrate in Higher Education; Filipino students overwhelmingly in vocational training; Colombian and Brazilian students follow a language-then-skills pathway; while Indian and Nepalese students split between degrees and VET.

Going one level deeper — into the subject studied rather than the level — shows specific pipelines between country and field. Management & Commerce dominates overall, but Information Technology is heavily India-driven and Health draws strongly on Filipino and Nepalese students. Several of these pipelines mirror Australian labour shortages.

05

Bangladesh quadrupled, China didn’t

Comparing 2019 with 2025 directly shows who recovered and who did not. Most top-ten sources grew — Bangladesh more than quadrupled — but China remains below its pre-COVID peak and Brazil is the only major source in sustained decline.

Finally, indexing each state to its own 2019 level (2019 = 100) lets us compare the shape of recovery without large states drowning out small ones. Most states overshot their pre-COVID baseline by 2024; New South Wales recovered the most modestly, while Western Australia shows the most dramatic rebound.

About this visualisation

Why & who

International education shapes the cities most Australians live in and is constantly in the political conversation, yet the underlying patterns are rarely shown. This page is built for a general Australian audience, avoiding jargon and statistical assumptions.

Data sources

Department of Education, International Student Data (January 2026 release), education.gov.au. Australian Bureau of Statistics, National, state and territory population (Sep 2025 quarter), abs.gov.au. World boundaries: vega-datasets world-110m.
